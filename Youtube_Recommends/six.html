YouTube is considered one of the first online streaming platforms, leading a major shift in how individuals viewed content at home. It was only two years after YouTube went live that Netflix shifted from mail-ordered DVD rentals to online streaming, (available for free for those already subscribed to the rental service.) Hulu followed shortly after, officially launching in 2008 along with Amazon Video On Demand (now Amazon Prime Video). Amazon originally had a service called Amazon Unbox which provided access to a large library of movies and television shows available for download over the internet, but Amazon Video On Demand provided content through online streaming rather than downloading. In 2010, Netflix started running their streaming service as its own platform and began producing original content in 2013 with their debut, House of Cards. From then, a gradual shift from cable TV to online streaming would take place until the streaming boom in 2019 and beyond, commonly referred to as the “streaming wars.” The battle of streaming platforms has been progressing over the course of this century, but 2019 marked a turning point in the rise of streaming platforms. Apple TV+ and Disney+ both launched that year, HBOMax and Peacock launched in 2020, and Paramount+ and Discovery+ launched in 2021, along with a substantial amount of other streaming services, as more people made the decision to “cut the cord,” aided largely by the 2020 pandemic. July 2022 marked the first month where streaming surpassed cable television in viewers. (Nielsen) But what exactly is streaming in the first place?
