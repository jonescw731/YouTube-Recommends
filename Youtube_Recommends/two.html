The First YouTube Video

Last month was the 20th anniversary of YouTube’s first video. Uploaded on April 23, 2005, the 18-second clip is titled “Me at the zoo” and features one of the original engineers behind YouTube, Jawed Karim, at the San Diego zoo, standing in front of the elephants. Karim was also the one to post the video onto the site. The three original developers—Karim, Steven Chen, and Chad Hurley—built YouTube to solve the issue of sharing videos online. Little did they know, YouTube would be at the forefront of a massive shift in internet culture and behavior. In just the first year after its launch, YouTube had already amassed over 25 million videos, with 20,000 videos being uploaded daily, and was streaming 15 million videos per day. (Hosch, The Youtube Team) Within barely a year of YouTube’s initial launch, Google bought the platform for roughly $1.65 billion. Today, over 2.5 million videos are uploaded to YouTube every single day—500 hours of content uploaded every minute—and the site averages 20 million daily users. (Stahl, Iqbal) For the first years of YouTube, they didn’t use any kind of recommendation algorithm to push videos. Instead, the videos on YouTube’s homepage were manually chosen by a group of editors entitled “community managers,” mostly referred to as the “coolhunters.” (Bergen) This team was charged with the task of perusing YouTube’s corpus of content to find a diverse collection of videos to post on YouTube’s homepage, updated throughout the day. Almost unfathomable to internet users today, for three years, the YouTube homepage looked the same for every person accessing the site. In 2007, both a “most viewed” and a “most liked” tab were added to the homepage, but it wasn’t until 2009 that a “recommended videos” box was introduced, and another two years for that recommendation box to become the majority of the YouTube homepage.
